1 · KeyGen SPEC §6 · commit-reveal Pedersen DKG

Every party deals a random polynomial (hash-committed first — R1 hashes are internal to the driver), shares go out point-to-point, and each dealt share is defended publicly against the commitment: a wrong deal is a complaint the dealer cannot defend. The joint key X = Σ xᵢ·G emerges; no one ever holds x.

2 · Beaver triple SPEC §7 · verifiable degree reduction

The committee produces (a, b, c) with c = a·b, nobody learning any of the three. Each party re-shares its local product and proves in one line — Chaum–Pedersen DLEQ (§7.3) — that its re-shared value really is the product of its dealt shares. A fake proof names the prover.

3 · Presignature SPEC §8 · the offline factory

The design-around: the inverse is dealt directly — [u] = [k⁻¹] is sampled as a random value and k is derived publicly, so no inversion protocol exists anywhere. Openings go through verified opening (commitment checks), and the nonce point R is pinned to the dealt sharing of k. Strictly single-use (§8.6).

4 · Sign SPEC §9 · one online round

One broadcast: party j sends s_j = m·u_j + r·z_j. Every share is checked against the public commitment m·A[u] + r·A[z] by point equality — a wrong share names its sender — and the valid shares interpolate to (r, s), an ordinary ECDSA signature under X.
